# Slide rules for a medical talk (one page)

Attach this file and `medsci_slides.pptx` to Claude or ChatGPT and ask:

> Follow the attached rules and use only the layouts in the attached template to build my slides.
> Kind of talk: (conference oral, 8 min / journal club / lecture, 30 min / case presentation)
> Audience: (specialists in my field / doctors from other fields / residents and students)
> What each slide should say: (a sentence or two each, below. If you give only a topic, attach the papers or material it should rest on)

Treat what the AI makes as 70 to 80 percent done. The rest is yours to look at and correct. Say it the way you would to a colleague: "use colour for emphasis", "narrow the first column of this table".

## 1. One point per slide

- One slide makes one claim. Two claims are two slides.
- The title is the slide's conclusion as a sentence, not a topic word: "Accuracy held in the external set", not "Results". Cut it until it fits on one line.
- Aim for 24-pt body text and never go under 20 pt. If it does not fit, cut words or split the slide.
- What you will say aloud goes in the speaker notes, not on the slide.

## 2. Figures and images large

- Where the figure or image is the point, it takes most of the slide.
- Images read on black (CT, MRI, angiography, ultrasound) go on a black layout. Plots, schematics and photographs drawn on white go on a white layout.
- Show where to look with one arrow and a short label.
- Do not paste a screenshot of a journal table. Redraw the rows and columns the talk needs, with three rules only (above the header, under it, under the last row).
- Draw plots as code (matplotlib, R) and insert the image. Do not let the AI build a chart out of shapes.
- A slide of text alone gets one small table or figure made from the study's data.

## 3. Emphasis and colour

- Emphasise with colour before bold. Colour one or two phrases a slide.
- Use at most two accent colours and keep their meaning through the deck: the first for the point of the slide, the second for what it is set against. In the template's colour picker they are Accent 1 and Accent 4.
- Colour is never the only carrier. To a colour-blind reader, and in a greyscale print, the two can look alike.

## 4. Leave out

These recur in decks made by AI.

- The same box on slide after slide; translucent panels, rounded corners, shadows
- A row of three or four oversized numbers with captions
- Every slide in one mould (title, three bullets, picture on the right)
- A logo, a long footer or a label above the title on every slide
- Icons and pictures that carry no information
- Stage directions: "Let us now look at", "In summary", "The key point is"
- Arrows with no stated meaning. An arrow is a claim ("causes", "is followed by")

A band across the top with the section name is fine if the speaker wants it. It is ordinary slide design.

## 5. For a medical talk

- Never generate a CT, MRI, histology or radiograph with AI, not even as an illustration.
- Remove names, numbers and dates from a patient image, in the pixels and in the file's metadata, before inserting it. A crop or a shape in PowerPoint leaves the original in the file.
- A number carries its unit, its confidence interval and its source.
- For a borrowed figure, check the licence article by article and credit it on the slide (`Fig. 2b · Author, Journal year`). Crop so that axes, legend and side markers stay.
- Compare each sentence the AI wrote about a paper with the paper itself. Left and right, anatomy and clinical content are checked by the speaker.
- Conflicts of interest and funding go on the Disclosure slide.
- The last slide is the conclusion in one line, not "Thank you". It stays up through the questions.

## 6. Choosing a layout in the template

| What the slide does | Layout |
|---|---|
| One number that is the finding | Key Finding |
| Design and participants | Study Design |
| A result as one figure | Result · Figure |
| A result as a table | Result · Table |
| Two things compared | Comparison |
| Limitations | Limitations |
| Three lines to take away | Take-home |
| One image and its findings | Imaging · Case |
| Several images | Imaging · 2×2, 1×3, 4 Steps |
| An image filling the screen | Imaging · Full |
| A question and its answer | Imaging · Quiz |

Do not draw shapes or boxes the template does not have.

## 7. After it is built

- Do the titles alone, read in order, tell the talk?
- Does any text run out of its box, overlap, or leave one word alone on a second line?
- Do more than three slides in a row share one mould?
- Where the figure is the point, is it more than half the screen?
- Open the file in PowerPoint and page through it. What the AI calls finished can look different on screen.

A deck that is easy to make does not shorten the preparation. The time saved on slides goes into studying the content and rehearsing the talk.
