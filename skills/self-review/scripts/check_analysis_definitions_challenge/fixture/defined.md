## Materials and Methods

### Study population
Adults without hypertension at a routine health examination between 2005 and 2015 were included.
Grouped by serum-marker quartile: Group A (<4.5), Group B (4.5–5.4), Group C (5.5–6.4), Group D (≥6.5 mg/dL).

### Statistical analysis
The outcome was incident hypertension, confirmed at a subsequent examination. Time to
event was measured from the baseline examination to the first qualifying examination; participants without
hypertension were censored at their last examination. Multivariable Cox proportional-hazards regression was used,
adjusted for age, sex, and body mass index. Because death precludes the outcome, a Fine–Gray
subdistribution hazard model was fitted for the same outcome, treating death as a competing event.
Discrimination and calibration were assessed against the observed hypertension status at five years, with
predicted versus observed risks compared across deciles. Two sensitivity analyses and a landmark analysis
were performed.

## Results

Median follow-up was 6.2 years. Compared with Group A, the adjusted hazard ratios were 1.28 for Group C and
1.57 for Group D. The Cox model showed a C-index of 0.68 and adequate calibration. The Fine–Gray model gave
similar subdistribution hazard ratios.

## Discussion

The groups behaved as expected.
