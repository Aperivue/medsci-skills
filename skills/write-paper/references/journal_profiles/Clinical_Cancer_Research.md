# Journal Profile: Clinical Cancer Research

## Journal Identity

- **Full name**: Clinical Cancer Research
- **Abbreviation**: Clin Cancer Res (CCR)
- **Publisher**: American Association for Cancer Research (AACR)
- **ISSN**: 1078-0432 (print), 1557-3265 (online)
- **Frequency**: Twice a month, 24 issues per year
- **Impact Factor**: 10.9 (JCR, as reported on the AACR metrics page; five-year 11.6; confirm current value)
- **Open Access**: Hybrid; standard copyright transfer or open access under CC BY-NC-ND or CC BY
- **Acceptance rate**: Not published by the journal
- **Peer review**: Single-blind

## Abstract Requirements

Research Articles require a structured 250-word abstract:

```text
Purpose
Experimental Design
Results
Conclusions
```

A separate **Translational Relevance** statement of 120-150 words is mandatory and sits between the title page and the abstract, describing how the results might apply to the future practice of cancer medicine. Do not cite references in the abstract.

---

## Required Sections (Research Article)

1. **Title page** with a running title of at most 60 characters, affiliations, and corresponding author details
2. **Translational Relevance** (120-150 words)
3. **Abstract** (250 words, structured)
4. **Introduction**
5. **Materials and Methods**, including a Statistical Analysis subsection; Methods text does not count toward the word limit
6. **Results**
7. **Discussion** (Results and Discussion must not be combined)
8. **Data Availability Statement** (mandatory, at the end of Materials and Methods)
9. **Code Availability Statement** where custom code was used
10. **Acknowledgments**, including AI disclosure where applicable
11. **References, tables, figure legends, figures**

Mandatory statements:
- Conflict-of-interest disclosure, or an explicit statement that the authors declare no potential conflicts; without it the manuscript does not enter peer review.
- Data integrity statement using the required wording, with at least one named author taking responsibility for data access, analysis accuracy, reporting, and responsible oversight of generative AI where relevant.
- IRB approval, Declaration of Helsinki compliance, and informed consent for human studies; IACUC approval for animal studies.
- Clinical trial registration in a WHO-recognized primary registry at or before enrollment, with the registration number.
- Author contributions using the CRediT taxonomy at revision.

## Statistical Reporting

- A Statistical Analysis section in Materials and Methods is mandatory.
- State n for each data point and clarify biological versus technical replicates.
- For n < 5, plot individual data points rather than error bars or confidence intervals.
- For n >= 5, the figure legend must state sample size, all statistical tests, all error bars, and confidence intervals.
- State whether samples were randomly assigned and describe the method; state whether experimenters were blinded to group assignment and outcome assessment.
- Justify sample size, or state how the size was chosen if no power calculation was done.
- Report exact p-values with ranges where appropriate, and confidence intervals where applicable.
- Report statistical software, version, and parameters; supply custom code in reproducible form with a Code Availability statement. Code Ocean is encouraged.
- Follow CONSORT, STROBE, PRISMA, and STARD as applicable.

## Figures

- **Maximum**: 6 figures and tables combined for Research Articles; 4 for Briefs; 8 for Resource Reports.
- **Resolution**: Not itemized on the AACR style page; use 300 dpi for halftones and 600 dpi for line art as the default.
- **Format**: Initial submission accepts PDF, JPG, or Word; revision requires EPS, TIFF, AI, PSD, PNG, PS, or high-resolution PDF.
- **Color**: Supply color in RGB, use distinct colors, and avoid red-green contrast for colorblind accessibility.
- **Legends**: Must state technical and biological replicate numbers, define symbols, include stains and magnifications, and include scale bars; state if an image is a representative example.
- **Panels**: Bold uppercase letters in sequence.
- **Images and blots**: Describe acquisition tools and processing in Methods; capture uncompressed (TIFF preferred); include controls and molecular weight markers; no vertical slicing without clear separation; retain at least six bandwidths above and below a cropped band; supply uncropped versions as supplemental data. Only linear whole-image adjustments are acceptable and must be disclosed.
- **Specific plots**: Waterfall plots need a response threshold line; Kaplan-Meier curves need number-at-risk tables; clinical trials need a CONSORT diagram.

## Common Rejection Reasons

_Editorial-risk summary drawn from the official guidance and stated editorial priorities._

1. Scope misfit: preclinical mechanism without a clinical bridge, or clinical description without translational insight.
2. Insufficient novelty, timeliness, or broad significance.
3. Missing conflict-of-interest statement, which blocks entry into peer review, or missing data-integrity, data-availability, ethics, or trial-registration statements.
4. Inadequate statistical and reproducibility reporting: unstated n, unexplained replicates, missing randomization or blinding description, or unjustified sample size.
5. Image and blot integrity problems, failure to meet word, reference, and figure limits, or combining Results with Discussion.

---

## Cover Letter

Should include:
- The clinical and translational significance and why the work fits CCR.
- Detailed description of any generative AI used to produce new text, images, or material.
- Disclosure of any related manuscript in press or under consideration elsewhere, with copies supplied; failure to disclose can delay review or cause rejection.
- Presubmission inquiries are accepted: for original research, a brief description of the study, its significance, the abstract, and prepared figures.

---

## AI Writing Disclosure Policy

- **Requirement level:** Mandatory disclosure when generative AI is used; not a blanket prohibition.
- **Permitted scope:** AI may revise or translate author-written text, disclosed in Acknowledgments. AI that produces new text, images, or material must be described in detail in the cover letter and the manuscript. AI used in the conduct of the research must be described in Methods.
- **Disclosure location:** Acknowledgments for all use, cover letter for AI-generated new content, Methods for research use.
- **AI-generated images:** Permitted if disclosed, with the tool credited in Acknowledgments.
- **Policy URL:** https://aacrjournals.org/pages/editorial-policies#ai

## Positioning

Submit here when the manuscript genuinely bridges laboratory and clinic: a clinical trial with mechanistic or correlative investigation, a biomarker with a defined clinical application, a resistance mechanism tied to patient samples, or a precision-oncology analysis with clear translational implications.

Do not submit here for a purely clinical outcomes analysis with no translational component, or for mechanistic work confined to cell lines and models without patient-level bridging. The mandatory Translational Relevance statement is a good self-test: if it cannot be written convincingly, the venue is probably wrong.
