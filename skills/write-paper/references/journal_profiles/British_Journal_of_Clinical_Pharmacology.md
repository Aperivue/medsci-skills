# Journal Profile: British Journal of Clinical Pharmacology

## Journal Identity

- **Full name**: British Journal of Clinical Pharmacology
- **Abbreviation**: BJCP
- **Publisher**: Wiley, on behalf of the British Pharmacological Society
- **ISSN**: 0306-5251 (print), 1365-2125 (online)
- **Frequency**: Monthly; online only ("It is online only ... and is published monthly")
- **Impact Factor**: Not stated on the journal's public pages consulted [TODO: verify at JCR]
- **Open Access**: Hybrid — a subscription journal with an open access option chosen after acceptance, subject to an article publication charge (amount not stated in the guidelines); CC-BY, CC-BY-NC or CC-BY-NC-ND. Articles that are not open access have no page, publication or colour charges.
- **Acceptance rate**: Not stated on the journal's public pages; the guidelines say "The rejection rate of BJCP is high."
- **Peer review**: Single-blind ("BJCP operates under a single-blind peer review model"); Editorial Office check, Senior Editor triage, then an Executive Editor assigns reviewers "until two good quality reviewers are received". Certain Phase 1, Phase 2 and experimental medicine manuscripts may go through an educational review programme (3-4 journal Editors with clinical pharmacology trainees, plus 1 external reviewer if needed); authors can opt out at submission.

Commentaries, Letters to the Editor and Essays need no title page. Commentaries and Letters should not contain new or unreviewed data. BJCP Series (Drugs and Therapeutics Committees, Clinical Pharmacology at Large, Twice Neglected, Concise Advice) accept original papers, review papers, short communications and commentaries. Free Format Submission applies at first submission; a manuscript invited for revision must then follow the full journal requirements.

## Abstract Requirements

Structured for Original Articles and Meta-analyses; unstructured (no sub-headings) for Short Communications, Review Articles, Case Reports and Essays.

```
Aim:
Methods:
Results:
Conclusion:
```

- **Original Article and Meta-analysis**: headings Aim, Methods, Results, Conclusion; 250 words maximum.
- **Content**: "estimates of relevant treatment effects, along with 95% CIs or other measures of variability, when appropriate, and confidence intervals (CI) of relevant differences between major end points."
- **Clinical trials**: the name of the trial register and the registration number go at the end of the abstract.
- **Bullet point summary (Original Articles and Short Communications)**: after the title page, two statements, each in up to 3 short bullet-point sentences (no more than 50 words): "What is already known about this subject" and "What this study adds".

## Required Sections (Original Article)

1. **Title page** — title no longer than 150 characters (including spaces) and without abbreviations; full names of the authors; institutional affiliations (footnote for a present address if different); a Principal Investigator statement for original articles and short reports where interventions were performed with human subjects/patients and or substances administered ("The authors confirm that the PI for this paper is X and that they had direct clinical responsibility for patients", or a statement that there is no PI); keywords; word count, table count, figure count.
2. **"What is already known about this subject" and "What this study adds"** statements.
3. **Abstract** — structured, as above.
4. **Main text** — Introduction, Methods, Results and Discussion. Include details of precision, accuracy, sensitivity and specificity for drug/metabolite assays. If a method or tool (software, questionnaire, scale) is used, state its licence and any permission obtained in the Materials subsection of the Methods.
5. **Acknowledgements** — including all financial and material support, CRO contributions to trials, and support with medical writing, PK, PD and statistical analysis.
6. **Conflict of interest statement**.
7. **Funding information** — all funding sources listed in the Acknowledgements section.
8. **Data availability statement** — required for all accepted manuscripts; confirm the presence or absence of shared data, with a persistent identifier if shared, or explain why the data cannot be shared.
9. **References** — any consistent style at submission; standardised to AMA style at publication.
10. **Tables** — at the end of the document, each with title and footnotes, in an editable format.
11. **Figure legends**.
12. **Appendices** — supplied as separate files, referred to in the text.

After acceptance, original articles, reviews and short reports are asked to hyperlink key ligands and targets, at first mention, to the BPS/IUPHAR Guide to Pharmacology (the guidelines also call it the Concise Guide to Pharmacology), and to send the editorial office a list of the hyperlinked ligands and targets; skip this if the article has no relevant key ligands or targets.

Figures, supplementary information and Graphical Abstracts are uploaded separately. Written in British English (Oxford spelling: "-ize" endings); no abbreviations section, and each abbreviation defined at first use; no footnotes to the text; SI units; rINN/pINN drug names (else BAN or chemical name); stereoisomers designated in the Methods (e.g. rac-propranolol, (S)-warfarin). Human-participant studies need a statement naming the approving ethics committee and the recognised standard followed (e.g. Declaration of Helsinki) in the Methods. Clinical trials must be prospectively registered in a publicly accessible database; if a trial is not registered, or was registered retrospectively, explain why.

Reporting guidelines: meta-analyses "should adhere to the PRISMA statement"; disproportionality analyses in an individual case safety reports database require a completed READUS-PV checklist as a condition of submission. Authors are "encouraged to adhere", where applicable, to CONSORT (including randomised crossover), SPIRIT, PRISMA, PRISMA-P, STROBE, CARE, COREQ, STARD and TRIPOD, CHEERS, the EQUATOR Network, ARRIVE, READUS-PV and the REFLECT statement, among others listed.

## Statistical Reporting

The guidelines state:

- Abstracts include estimates of relevant treatment effects with 95% CIs or other measures of variability, when appropriate, and CIs of relevant differences between major end points.
- In tables, footnote symbols a, b, c are used in that order, and *, **, *** are reserved for P-values; statistical measures such as SD or SEM are identified in the headings.
- Details of precision, accuracy, sensitivity and specificity are given for drug/metabolite assays.

## Figures

- **Maximum**: Original Articles, Review Articles and Meta-analyses — preferably no more than 8 figures and tables; Short Communications and Case Reports — no more than 3 figures or tables; Commentaries and Letters — no more than 1 figure or table.
- **Resolution**: No DPI minimum is stated in the guidelines text consulted; for peer review "a wide variety of formats, sizes, and resolutions are accepted", with Wiley's basic and post-acceptance figure requirements linked.
- **Format**: Figures submitted as separate files; legends concise but comprehensive, defining all symbols, abbreviations and units.
- **Color**: Colour figures are reproduced in colour online free of charge; line figures (graphs and charts) are preferably supplied in black and white. Avoid palettes that are difficult for readers with colour-blindness, such as green/red.
- **Integrity**: After acceptance all figures undergo a Wiley integrity check; raw data may be requested.
- **Graphical abstract**: strongly encouraged for original articles and reviews, using the templates supplied during submission.

## Common Rejection Reasons

1. Out of scope — purely basic pharmacology papers, or work without a major clinical component; Senior Editor triage may return out-of-scope manuscripts, and BJCP may offer transfer to British Journal of Pharmacology.
2. Pharmacokinetics only — papers on a new medicine that report pharmacokinetics without a measure of pharmacodynamics (e.g. a first-in-human study reporting only PK and side effects when quantitative PD could have been reported).
3. Principal Investigator not an author — BJCP "has a strict policy of not accepting original articles when the GCP-defined PI of the study is not an author"; if the PI is not listed, "a clear explanation must be supplied with your submission".
4. Case Reports with only routine clinical data — they need mechanistic, pharmacokinetic and/or pharmacodynamic data.
5. Disproportionality analyses (e.g. FAERS) without a READUS-PV checklist, with causal language, or without a highly original contribution.
6. Novelty and impact — "Quality papers are rejected on the basis of novelty or perceived potential impact, or are studies deemed out of scope, or which show negative results" (such papers may be referred to Pharmacology Research & Perspectives).
7. Incomplete submission — missing required documentation is returned by the Editorial Office; poor English may lead to return for revision.

## Cover Letter

The guidelines consulted state no cover-letter requirements. Items the guidelines ask authors to supply with the submission:

- Justification when an Original Article significantly exceeds 4,000 words.
- A clear explanation if the Principal Investigator is not listed as an author.
- Up to two suggested reviewers (names, institutions and institutional email addresses); opposed reviewers may also be given.
- For a compound of undisclosed structure, contact the Editorial Office (BJCPedoffice@wiley.com) before submitting, giving the reasons.

## AI Writing Disclosure Policy

- **Requirement level:** Required — "The use of such tools must be described in detail in the Methods or Acknowledgements section."
- **Permitted scope:** "The BJCP permits the use of AIGC tools in the writing and construction of your article." Tools used to improve spelling, grammar and general editing are outside the scope of the policy. AIGC tools cannot be listed as authors (per COPE). The author is accountable for all AI-produced content, and the editor makes the final decision on whether a tool's use is appropriate.
- **Disclosure location:** Methods or Acknowledgements section.
- **AI-generated images:** Not specified in the journal's AI section.

## Positioning

Submit here for clinical pharmacology in humans: first-in-human and Phase 1 studies with pharmacodynamic measures, clinical PK/PD, experimental medicine and therapeutics, precision medicine, clinical toxicology, drug safety (including disproportionality analyses), prescribing, and clinical pharmacology education, for a readership of clinical pharmacologists, pharmacists, physicians and pharmaceutical-industry scientists. Do not submit purely basic pharmacology (BJCP's Senior Editors may offer to transfer out-of-scope manuscripts to its sister journal, British Journal of Pharmacology), pharmacokinetics-only studies of a new medicine, or case reports with only routine clinical data.
