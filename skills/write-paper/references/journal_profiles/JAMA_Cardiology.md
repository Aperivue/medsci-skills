# Journal Profile: JAMA Cardiology

## Journal Identity

- **Full name**: JAMA Cardiology
- **Abbreviation**: JAMA Cardiol
- **Publisher**: American Medical Association (AMA)
- **ISSN**: 2380-6583 (print), 2380-6591 (online)
- **Frequency**: Published online weekly, every Wednesday; 12 print/online issues a year
- **Impact Factor**: ~15.2 (2025 Journal Impact Factor; journal's For Authors page, updated November 2026)
- **Open Access**: Hybrid — subscription journal; authors of eligible research articles may elect to pay for immediate open access on the day of publication; all research articles are made free access online 12 months after publication; all published research articles are deposited in PubMed Central
- **Acceptance rate**: ~8% for all submissions (journal's For Authors page)
- **Peer review**: Single-anonymized (single-blind) — "The journal uses a single-anonymized peer review process: peer reviewer identities are kept confidential ...; author identities are made known to reviewers"; median time to first decision 5 days without external peer review, 37 days with review

## Abstract Requirements

Structured abstract for reports of original data, meta-analyses, and systematic reviews; no abstract for Editorials, Viewpoints, and special features. No information in the abstract that does not appear in the text.

```
Importance:
Objective:
Design:
Setting:
Participants:
Intervention(s) [clinical trials] / Exposure(s) [observational studies]:
Main Outcome(s) and Measure(s):
Results:
Conclusions and Relevance:
Trial Registration: [clinical trials only]
```

- **Reports of Original Data**: headings above; 350 words maximum. Design, Setting, and Participants are kept separate at submission (combined only during editing). Trial registration (registry name, number, URL) at the end of the abstract for clinical trials.
- **Meta-analysis**: Importance, Objective, Data Sources, Study Selection, Data Extraction and Synthesis, Main Outcome(s) and Measure(s), Results, Conclusions and Relevance; 350 words maximum.
- **Systematic Review**: Importance, Objective, Evidence Review, Findings, Conclusions and Relevance; 350 words maximum.
- **Narrative Review**: 3-part structured abstract — Importance, Observations, Conclusions and Relevance; 300 words maximum.

## Required Sections (Original Article)

1. **Title page** — title no longer than 100 characters (including spaces) for reports of research; full names, highest academic degrees, and affiliations of all authors; corresponding author name and complete contact information; manuscript word count (not including title, abstract, acknowledgment, references, tables, and figure legends). For clinical trials, meta-analyses, and systematic reviews, include the study type as a subtitle (e.g. "A Randomized Clinical Trial").
2. **Key Points** — before the Abstract; required for research and review manuscripts; 75-100 words; Question (1 sentence), Findings (1-2 sentences, design and primary outcome, no statistical tests or measures of variance), Meaning (1 sentence).
3. **Abstract** — structured, as above.
4. **Introduction** — brief; end with the explicit objective.
5. **Methods** — ethics: IRB/ethics committee approval (or waiver) and informed consent described; study design and participants (inclusion/exclusion, recruitment dates); interventions or exposures; outcomes (primary and secondary defined before data collection); statistical analysis (tests, a priori significance levels, 1- vs 2-sided, software with version and manufacturer). Trial registration, protocol, and CONSORT checklist for randomized trials; EQUATOR reporting guidelines throughout.
6. **Results** — begin with participant flow and baseline characteristics (no P values in baseline tables for parallel-group randomized trials); report absolute numbers/rates with 95% CIs; intention-to-treat analysis for randomized trials.
7. **Discussion** — interpret findings, including limitations.
8. **Conclusions** — brief; only conclusions directly supported by the results.
9. **Data Sharing Statement** — required for research manuscripts.
10. **Study Type** — stated for research manuscripts.
11. **Acknowledgment** — funding/support, author contributions, conflicts of interest (ICMJE disclosures), and AI-use disclosure where applicable.
12. **References** — AMA style; superscript arabic numerals in order of appearance; list all authors up to 6, then first 3 followed by "et al."; journal references include the issue number in parentheses after the volume number.

Manuscript file: title page, abstract, text, references, and as appropriate figure legends and tables, each starting on a new page. Figures submitted as separate files (1 file per figure). Word document (not PDF); 10-, 11-, or 12-point font, double-spaced.

## Statistical Reporting

The guidelines state:

- Report absolute numbers and/or rates with 95% CIs; avoid redundant reporting of relative data. Means with SDs for normally distributed data; medians with ranges or IQRs for skewed data.
- P values should follow the reporting of comparisons of absolute numbers or rates and measures of uncertainty, never presented alone: for P < .001 report as "P<.001"; for P between .001 and .01 report to the nearest thousandth; for P ≥ .01 report to the nearest hundredth; for P > .99 report as "P>.99". Exponentially small P values (e.g. genetic association studies) may use exponents (e.g. P = 1×10−5). Test statistics and degrees of freedom generally need not be presented.
- Measures of relative risk (relative risk, hazard ratios, odds ratios) should include confidence intervals; plot ratio data on a log scale without log-transforming the values. Diagnostic-test studies should report sensitivity, specificity, and likelihood ratios (plus prevalence/pretest likelihood if predictive value or accuracy is reported).
- Randomized trials: intention-to-treat as the primary analysis; deviations described as "modified intention-to-treat". Prespecify the primary outcome(s); for more than 1 primary outcome, corrections for multiple testing should generally be used; secondary/subgroup/post hoc analyses should address multiplicity or be described as exploratory and hypothesis-generating.
- Statistical Analysis subsection at the end of Methods: tests used, a priori significance levels, whether tests were 1- or 2-sided, and the statistical software used including version and manufacturer, plus extension packages. Do not describe software commands.
- Analyses should follow EQUATOR reporting guidelines and be consistent with the protocol and statistical analysis plan.

## Figures

- **Maximum**: Original Investigations, Systematic Reviews, Narrative Reviews — no more than 5 tables and/or figures combined; Special Communications — no more than 4; Research Letters — no more than 2 small tables and/or figures.
- **Resolution**: Illustrations and photographs (including clinical images) ≥350 ppi; line drawings ≥600 ppi. Images must meet the minimum resolution at creation; electronically increasing resolution afterwards is not acceptable.
- **Format**: Vector files preferred (.ai, .eps, .pdf); each figure uploaded as a separate file, no more than 1 MB per figure at initial submission. Figures are re-created by journal graphics experts to JAMA Network style before publication; online-only figures are not re-created.
- **Color**: Color photographs in RGB mode (Adobe RGB or sRGB); black-and-white photographs in RGB or grayscale. Colors must be distinguishable.
- **Rules**: No pie charts, 3-D graphs, or stacked bar charts (except comparisons of distributions of ordinal data); no more than 4 figure panels unless justified; error bars in both directions unless only 1-sided variability was calculated; survival plots must show the number at risk at intervals along the x-axis; primary outcome data must not be presented in figures alone — exact values with measures of variability belong in the text or a table and in the Abstract. Figure titles no longer than 10-15 words; legends define all symbols, abbreviations, and units.

## Common Rejection Reasons

1. Insufficient priority — "Manuscripts with insufficient priority for publication are rejected promptly" at editorial triage; the journal is highly selective (8% acceptance).
2. Missing required elements — no Key Points box, no structured abstract, no Data Sharing Statement, or (for trials) no trial registration, protocol, or CONSORT checklist.
3. Reporting-guideline non-compliance — CONSORT for trials, PRISMA/MOOSE for meta-analyses, STROBE for observational studies, CHEERS for economic evaluations, and other EQUATOR checklists not followed.
4. Undisclosed or impermissible AI use — AI-drafted Opinion manuscripts, Letters to the Editor, or Online Comments are not permitted; failure to disclose AI use can lead to rejection or postpublication corrective action; AI must not be used to generate or format references.
5. Primary outcomes shown only in figures, or the same data duplicated across figures and tables.
6. Case reports or images without patient permission — Clinical Challenge pieces and identifiable images require a signed patient permission form.

## Cover Letter

The guidelines consulted state:

- Include a cover letter and complete contact information for the corresponding author (affiliation, postal/mail address, email address, and telephone number).
- State whether the authors have published, posted, or submitted any related papers from the same study (see Previous Publication, Related Manuscripts and Reports, and Preprints).
- Authors are encouraged to submit previous peer-review and editorial comments in full, with their responses, as a Previous Peer Review and Editorial Comments file.

## AI Writing Disclosure Policy

- **Requirement level:** Required — "Use of AI, LLMs, chatbots, or similar technologies to assist with content creation, revision, and formatting must be reported in the Acknowledgment section." Failure to disclose, if detected during editorial review or after publication, can lead to rejection or postpublication corrective action.
- **Permitted scope:** AI may assist with content creation, revision, and formatting if disclosed; authors must review and confirm the accuracy of all AI-generated content and take full responsibility for it. AI-created content is discouraged unless part of formal research design or methods, and then only with a clear description of the content created and the name of the model or tool, version and extension numbers, and manufacturer. AI must not be used to generate or format references. AI must not be used to draft Opinion manuscripts, Letters to the Editor, or Online Comments. Basic grammar and spelling checkers need no disclosure.
- **Disclosure location:** Acknowledgment section (Methods section when AI is part of formal research design or methods) — name of the AI software platform/program/tool, version and extension numbers, manufacturer, date(s) of use, a brief description of how the AI was used and on which portions of the manuscript, and confirmation that the author(s) take responsibility for the content.
- **AI-generated images:** Not permitted — clinical images and clinical illustrations created or manipulated by AI, machine learning tools, or similar technologies are not permitted unless part of formal research design or methods, which must be fully disclosed. The same applies to AI-created video and audio.
- **Policy URL:** https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamacardiology/pages/instructions-for-authors (section "Use of AI in Publication and Research"; no dedicated journal AI policy page)

## Positioning

Submit here for clinical cardiology research that can change practice or inform guidelines: randomized and nonrandomized trials, cohort and case-control studies, diagnostic and prognostic studies, meta-analyses and systematic reviews, health-services and outcomes research, and health policy work, for a readership of clinical investigators, clinicians, and trainees in cardiovascular medicine worldwide. Health equity, when supported by original science, is a leading editorial priority. Do not submit basic or mechanistic work without a clinical link, or papers whose findings have no plausible path to changing what cardiologists do; the journal is positioned "at the nexus of clinical investigation, actionable clinical science, and clinical practice".
