# Journal Profile: Journal of the American College of Cardiology

## Journal Identity

- **Full name**: Journal of the American College of Cardiology
- **Abbreviation**: JACC
- **Publisher**: Elsevier (on behalf of the American College of Cardiology)
- **ISSN**: 0735-1097 (print), 1558-3597 (online)
- **Frequency**: Weekly
- **Impact Factor**: 22.3 (2025 JIF, per the journal's ScienceDirect page)
- **Open Access**: Hybrid — subscription journal with optional open access; APC USD 7,525 (excluding taxes). Subscription: no publication fee charged to authors, and published articles are immediately available to subscribers.
- **Acceptance rate**: Not published
- **Peer review**: Single-blind — the authors are blinded to the identity of the reviewers, and as a general rule reviewers are blinded to each other. The Associate Editor is identified at the end of the review process. All reviewers and editors must report potential conflicts of interest; when one exists the manuscript is reassigned.

No more than two corresponding authors and no more than two joint authors in any position for State-of-the-Art Reviews, Review Topics of the Week, and Original Investigations. Cardiovascular Medicine and Society, Research Letters, and Letters to the Editor allow no more than 10, 10, and 5 authors respectively, with no joint authorship permitted. Proposals for State-of-the-Art Reviews and Review Topics of the Week should first be emailed to the editorial office at jacc@acc.org; most reviews are solicited. In general, case reports will not be considered for publication. Fellows-in-Training & Early Career articles must be by authors within 7 years of medical training, are limited to 3 authors, and should be emailed to jacc@acc.org rather than submitted online. Letters to the Editor must be submitted within 3 weeks of the print issue date of the article, concern Original Investigations only (not reviews, editorials, correspondence, research letters, or guidelines), and carry a unique title of 15 words or less that does not include the title of the original paper.

## Abstract Requirements

**Structured abstract for Original Investigations, 250 words maximum, with 5 explicit headings:**

```
Background: [Context and knowledge gap]
Objectives: [Specific aim of the study]
Methods: [Design, setting, population, exposures, outcomes, analytic approach]
Results: [Primary results with effect sizes and P values; N analyzed]
Conclusions: [Direct answer to the objective]
```

The abstract must present essential data in 5 paragraphs using complete sentences; all data in the abstract must also appear in the manuscript text or tables. Review articles (State-of-the-Art Reviews, Review Topics of the Week) take an unstructured abstract of no more than 150 words. Original Investigations and reviews also require a **condensed abstract of no more than 100 words, stressing clinical implications**. Cardiovascular Medicine and Society submissions require no abstract. Immediately after the abstract, provide a maximum of **6 key words** (American spelling, avoiding general and plural terms, multiple concepts, and words already used in the title) and a list of up to **10 abbreviations** on a separate page following the abstract.

## Required Sections (Original Article)

1. **Title page** — title (no more than 15 words) and brief title (no more than 7 words); total word count; authors' names with degrees; departments and institutions; funding (grants, contracts, financial support, with cities and states of foundations, funds, and institutions); disclosures (each author's relationships with industry within the past 2 years, or a statement that none exist); corresponding author contact information (full postal address, telephone, fax, email, and a Twitter handle if available); a short tweet of approximately 150 characters summarizing the paper; acknowledgements (100 words or less).
2. **Abstract** (structured, 250 words) and **Condensed Abstract** (100 words).
3. **Key Words** (maximum 6) and **Abbreviations** (up to 10).
4. **Text** — Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion, Conclusions. Use headings and subheadings in the Methods, Results, and particularly the Discussion. Every reference, figure, and table must be cited in the text in numerical order according to order of mention. Study limitations are required at the end of the Discussion. The manuscript (including tables) is uploaded as a Microsoft Word document, 1-inch margins, 12-point Times New Roman; title and abstract pages single-spaced, all text from the introduction onward double-spaced.
5. **Clinical Perspectives** (Original Investigations only, on a separate page after the Conclusions) — **Clinical Competencies** (implications of the study for current practice, mapped to the 6 ACGME competency domains: Medical Knowledge, Patient Care and Procedural Skills, Interpersonal and Communication Skills, Systems-Based Practice, Practice-Based Learning, and Professionalism) and **Translational Outlook** (future directions and next steps). Review articles instead provide 3–4 highlight bullet points (85 characters / 15 words or fewer per bullet).
6. **References** — numbered consecutively in the order mentioned in the text; see Statistical Reporting and figure guidance below for citation style.
7. **Figure titles and captions**, including a title and caption for the Central Illustration (listed last unless it is an existing figure).
8. **Tables**, each on a separate page.
9. **Figures** and **Supplemental material** (all supplemental materials except videos uploaded as one separately uploaded Word document labeled Supplemental Appendix).

## Statistical Reporting

The journal states that all publishable manuscripts are reviewed for appropriateness and accuracy of statistical methods and statistical interpretation, following the statistics section of the ICMJE Recommendations. In the Methods section, provide a subsection detailing the statistical methods, including the specific methods used to summarize the data, the methods used for hypothesis testing (if any), and the level of significance used. When using more sophisticated statistical methods (beyond t-tests, chi-square, and simple linear regression), specify the statistical package, version number, and nondefault options used.

## Figures

- **Format:** TIF. Graphics software such as Photoshop or Illustrator should be used to create art, not presentation software such as PowerPoint, CorelDraw, or Harvard Graphics.
- **Resolution:** color and gray-scale images at least 300 DPI; line art (black and white or color) and combinations of gray-scale images and line art at least 1,200 DPI. Lettering should be of sufficient size to be legible after reduction (optimal size 12 points); figures should be no smaller than 13 cm × 18 cm. There is no fee for the publication of color figures.
- **Legends:** figure legends should be an in-depth explanation of each figure, including a figure title and a caption (2–3 sentences) covering the purpose of the figure and brief method, results, and discussion statements. Target length 50–100 words per figure. All abbreviations used in a figure must be identified in the legend or in alphabetical order at the end of each legend; all symbols must be explained. Legends are double-spaced on pages separate from the text.
- **Numbering:** all figures must have a number, title, and caption, and must be cited in numerical order in the text. Supplemental figures are cited as "Supplemental Figure 1", "Supplemental Figure 2", etc.
- **Central Illustration:** all Original Investigations, State-of-the-Art Reviews, and Review Topics of the Week must develop at least 1 Central Illustration (which may be a hand-drawn figure) summarizing the entire manuscript or a major section of it. The journal's in-house medical illustrators create the final printable versions in consultation with the authors and editors. The Central Illustration legend is listed last in the figure legends unless it is an existing figure.
- **Tables:** each table on a separate page, table number and title centered above the table, explanatory notes below. Use Arabic numbers; table numbers must correspond with the order cited in the text. Tables must be self-explanatory and must not duplicate data in the text or figures. Abbreviations are listed in a footnote under the table in alphabetical order; footnote symbols appear in the order *, †, ‡, §, ‖, #, **, ††, etc.
- **Permissions:** if previously published figures or tables are used, written permission from the original publisher (or copyright holder) is required and the source must be cited in the legend or table footnote.
- **Videos:** at the editors' discretion; MP4 files only, no larger than 15 MB, with a video legends page immediately after the figure legends page and a thumbnail image for each video (cropped to W 200 × H 150, uploaded as a TIFF file).

## Common Rejection Reasons

- **Out of scope or insufficient priority.** At initial submission the manuscript is checked for compliance and legibility, then assigned to the Editor-in-Chief and an Associate Editor, who decides whether it is sent for peer review or is not of sufficient priority for JACC. All Associate Editors review completed submissions in a weekly meeting and decide Accept, Minor Revision, Revision Required, or Reject.
- **No clear clinical relevance.** JACC publishes experimental investigations with clear clinical relevance and novel translational studies with direct clinical relevance that significantly advance the field; purely mechanistic work without a patient-facing implication does not fit.
- **Case report.** In general, case reports will not be considered for publication.
- **Missing Central Illustration or Clinical Perspectives.** Original Investigations require both; submissions without them are incomplete.
- **Word-count overrun.** The 5,000-word cap for Original Investigations includes text, references, and figure legends; the 10,000-word cap for State-of-the-Art Reviews includes references and figure legends.
- **Missing disclosures.** Every author must disclose relationships with industry and other relevant entities (financial or otherwise) within the past 2 years on the title page, or state that none exist; all authors must electronically sign a Relationship with Industry form, and authors cannot be added after acceptance or on proofs.
- **Letter to the Editor out of scope.** Letters are considered only on Original Investigation articles, not on review articles, editorials, correspondence, research letters, or guidelines, and must arrive within 3 weeks of the print issue date.
- **Preprint conditions unmet.** Preprint posting is not prior publication only if the authors acknowledge the deposition with accession numbers or DOIs at submission, the peer-review-altered version is not deposited, the preprint is not indexed in MEDLINE or PubMed, and the archived preprint is updated with the published DOI and link on publication.

## Cover Letter

A short paragraph telling the editors why the authors think their paper merits publication may be included in the cover letter. Potential reviewers may be suggested in the cover letter, as well as reviewers to avoid; final reviewer assignment is determined by the editors. Authors seeking **expedited review** for Original Investigations reporting important original findings of high-potential clinical impact or research significance should request it, with the rationale, in the cover letter at the time of submission: the editors commit to a decision regarding suitability for expedited processing within 2 days and an initial decision within 14 days. Manuscripts not deemed appropriate for the expedited track are considered under the standard review process. Appeals of editorial decisions are made by email to jacc@acc.org within 30 days of the decision, addressing all reviewers' concerns; the editors' decision on appeal is final.

## AI Writing Disclosure Policy

The journal's guide displays no journal-specific AI policy; **Elsevier publisher-level policy applies** (Elsevier, Generative AI policies for journals, https://www.elsevier.com/en-in/about/policies-and-standards/generative-ai-policies-for-journals, opened 2026-11-21):

- **Permitted scope:** AI tools may support manuscript preparation (summarizing literature, organizing content, improving language, clarity, and readability, checking completeness against guidelines) provided authors maintain full oversight and responsibility. AI must not replace the authors' own scientific judgement, analysis, interpretation, or intellectual contribution, and must not be used to generate sections of a manuscript without genuine intellectual contribution, or to fabricate or alter research data, results, or references.
- **Disclosure location:** authors must include a separate declaration section at the end of the manuscript, immediately before the references, titled "Declaration of generative AI and AI-assisted technologies in the manuscript preparation process." The statement names the tool used, the purpose of its use, and confirms the authors reviewed the output and take full responsibility for the content. Suggested wording: "During the preparation of this work the author(s) used [NAME OF TOOL/SERVICE] in order to [REASON]. After using this tool/service, the author(s) reviewed and edited the content as needed and take(s) full responsibility for the content of the publication." Basic spelling, grammar, and punctuation checks, basic reference-manager functions, and assistive technology used solely for accessibility do not require disclosure.
- **AI in research:** where AI tools form part of the research design or methods (including AI-assisted imaging for data collection or interpretation, or predictive modelling), the use must be described in a reproducible manner in the Methods section, including the name of the model or tool, the version used, and the developer or manufacturer where applicable. AI-assisted writing of code must be declared in detail in the Methods section.
- **AI-generated images:** AI tools must not be used to create or alter images that represent primary observed or experimental data (including adjustments to brightness, contrast, or color balance, which should only be done with established image-processing software). Data visualizations may be AI-supported only when the visual output is directly derived from underlying data through reproducible analytical, computational, or statistical methods clearly reported in the Methods section, with the model or tool, version, and developer disclosed there. Explanatory images (flow charts, decision trees, timelines, schematic illustrations) may be AI-supported, with the use disclosed in the caption of each image (tool, version, how used) and in the general AI declaration. General-purpose generative AI image tools must not be used to create graphical abstracts; dedicated scientific illustration tools should be used instead. AI-generated cover art requires prior permission from the journal editor and publisher.
- **Authorship:** AI tools must not be listed as an author or co-author, or cited as an author; authorship implies responsibilities and tasks that can only be attributed to and performed by humans.

## Positioning

Submit here for clinical cardiology research that can change practice or inform ACC/AHA guidelines: randomized and nonrandomized trials, large cohort and registry studies, imaging and interventional advances with demonstrated patient benefit, prevention and risk-stratification work, and expert consensus documents, for a readership of practicing cardiologists and cardiovascular researchers worldwide. The mandatory Central Illustration and Clinical Perspectives sections force a crisp statement of what should change in practice, and the expedited track suits late-breaking trial work. Do not submit purely mechanistic basic science without a clinical implication, single-centre observational work with limited generalizability, or case reports; those belong in a basic-science journal or a JACC specialty journal, to which JACC can offer transfer with its reviews.
