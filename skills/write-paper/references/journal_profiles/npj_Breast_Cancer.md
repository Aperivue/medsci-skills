## Abstract Requirements

Articles require an **unstructured** abstract of at most 150 words with no subheadings. The title must be 15 words or fewer and free of punctuation, idioms, and puns.

---

## Required Sections (Article)

1. **Title page**: title of 15 words or fewer, full author list, affiliations, corresponding author contact
2. **Abstract** (150 words, no subheadings)
3. **Introduction** (no subheadings)
4. **Results** (subheadings should be used)
5. **Discussion** (no subheadings, no separate limitations section, no conclusions section)
6. **Methods** (subheadings should be used), including a Statistics and Reproducibility section
7. **Data Availability** (mandatory, after Methods and before References)
8. **Code Availability** (where applicable)
9. **Acknowledgments** (funding is declared here; a separate funding statement is not used)
10. **Author Contributions** (mandatory, by author initials)
11. **Competing Interests** (mandatory, financial and non-financial)
12. **References**
13. **Figure legends** (at most 350 words per figure)

Mandatory items:
- Ethics approval and consent details in Methods for studies involving humans, human material, or human data; animal ethics where applicable.
- Trial registration number and date in Methods for clinical trials.
- A completed Nature Portfolio Reporting Summary / editorial policy checklist with the revised manuscript, published as a supplementary file.
- CONSORT checklist or extension in Supplementary Information for randomized trials; ARRIVE Essential 10 strongly encouraged for preclinical animal studies.
- Systematic reviews, scoping reviews, and meta-analyses must be submitted as Articles rather than Reviews.

## Statistical Reporting

The Methods must include a **Statistics and Reproducibility** section stating:
- The name of each statistical test and why it is appropriate, including normality assumptions.
- The n value for each analysis and the comparisons of interest.
- The alpha level and whether tests were one- or two-tailed.
- The actual p-value for each test, not merely "significant" or "P < 0.05", with clear attribution of which test produced which p-value.

Data presentation requirements:
- Summarize datasets with n, a clearly labeled measure of centre (mean or median), and a clearly labeled measure of variability (standard deviation or range); prefer ranges for datasets smaller than about 10.
- Label error bars clearly and state whether values after a plus-or-minus sign are s.e.m. or s.d.
- Show the full data distribution: overlay individual points on bar charts or use dot plots or box-and-whisker plots.
- Address multiple comparisons, normality testing, and small-sample test choice explicitly.
- Name all statistical software; custom code central to the conclusions must be available to editors and reviewers, with a Code Availability statement.

## Figures

- **Maximum**: Not stated.
- **Resolution**: Minimum 300 dpi or higher, RGB preferred; peer-review versions need only be legible.
- **Format**: Vector preferred (.ai, .eps, .pdf, .ps, .svg); layered .psd or .tif; bitmap .psd, .tif, .png, .jpg; .ppt if fully editable. Video up to 150 MB per file.
- **Color**: RGB; avoid red-green contrast and rainbow scales; recoloring primary data to color-safe combinations is strongly encouraged.
- **Type and layout**: Sans-serif (Arial or Helvetica), optimized to 8 pt at print size, lines at least 1 point, A4 page width, white background. Avoid 3D effects and truncated histogram axes. Use scale bars rather than magnification factors.
- **Panels**: Multi-panel figures in a single file labeled a), b), c) in lowercase bold.
- **Legends**: Brief title, description of each panel and symbol, meaning and calculation of error bars, at most 350 words.

## Common Rejection Reasons

_Editorial-risk summary drawn from the official guidance and stated editorial priorities._

1. Insufficient novelty or significance; the journal seeks important advances that bring new insight to a specialized area.
2. Technical quality concerns, particularly weak statistical design or unclear replication.
3. Missing mandatory items: data availability, code availability, ethics and consent, trial registration, author contributions, competing interests, or the Reporting Summary.
4. Statistics and Reproducibility reporting that omits exact p-values, n values, alpha, or test justification.
5. Structural noncompliance, such as subheadings in the Discussion, a separate conclusions or limitations section, a structured abstract, or a title longer than 15 words.

## Cover Letter

Must include:
- Corresponding author affiliation and contact information.
- A brief discussion of the context and importance of the work.
- Why the work is appropriate for npj Breast Cancer, without repeating the abstract and introduction.

May also include, confidentially: competing interests, the status of related work in press or submitted elsewhere, suggested referees considering diversity of gender, ethnicity, geography, and career stage, and named excluded referees. The cover letter is not shared with referees.

## AI Writing Disclosure Policy

- **Requirement level:** Nature Portfolio tiered framework. Assistive use is permitted with recommended disclosure; evaluative or interpretive use is permitted only with disclosure and demonstrable human oversight; use that replaces human judgment is not permitted.
- **Permitted scope:** Language polishing, structuring, translation, data cleaning, and similar assistive tasks are green-tier. Suggesting analytic approaches, drafting explanatory summaries, and extensive copy editing are amber-tier and require verification and accountability. Generating hypotheses, analyses, or conclusions presented as human-derived, fabricating data or citations, and delegating peer review are prohibited.
- **Disclosure location:** Methods section, or a suitable alternative if there is no Methods section.
- **AI-generated images:** Photorealistic AI images and deepfakes are not permitted.
- **Policy URL:** https://www.nature.com/nature-portfolio/editorial-policies/ai

## Positioning

Submit here for breast cancer research that delivers a specialized advance with new insight: molecular classification, translational biomarker work, genomics, metastasis mechanisms, immunotherapy and treatment biology, imaging, screening and prevention, survivorship, or well-designed clinical research with a mechanistic or precision-medicine angle.

Do not choose npj Breast Cancer for a purely descriptive regional outcomes series with limited novelty; a breast-specialty or society journal is a better fit. Note the APC and the Nature Portfolio format rules, which differ substantially from the IMRAD conventions of most clinical oncology journals.
