# Journal Profile: Journal of Breast Cancer

## Journal Identity

- **Full name**: Journal of Breast Cancer
- **Abbreviation**: J Breast Cancer
- **Publisher**: Korean Breast Cancer Society
- **ISSN**: 1738-6756 (print), 2092-9900 (online)
- **Frequency**: Bimonthly (last day of February, April, June, August, October, December)
- **Impact Factor**: 2.4 (2024, as displayed on the journal homepage; confirm current value)
- **Open Access**: Full OA, CC BY-NC 4.0; article processing charge applies
- **Acceptance rate**: Not published by the journal
- **Peer review**: Double-blind; initial editorial assessment followed by two to three external reviewers

## Abstract Requirements

Original Articles require a structured abstract of no more than 300 words:

```text
Purpose
Methods
Results
Conclusion
```

Provide five or fewer MeSH keywords in alphabetical order.

---

## Required Sections (Original Article)

1. **Title page** (submitted separately for double-blind review)
   - Manuscript type, title (20 words or fewer, no abbreviations), authors with ORCID IDs and institutions
   - Corresponding author contact details
   - Running title (10 words or fewer)
   - Conference presentation, funding, and conflicts of interest
2. **Abstract and keywords**
3. **Introduction**
4. **Methods**
   - IRB/ethics approval and informed consent where applicable
   - Clinical-trial registration details for clinical trials
5. **Results**
6. **Discussion**
7. **Acknowledgements**
8. **References**
9. **Tables and figures**

The anonymized main text must not name authors or institutions.

---

## Statistical Reporting

- Italicize lower-case *p*.
- Use SI units and Arabic numerals.
- Define abbreviations at first use and use them sparingly.
- For study protocols, state the statistical analysis method including a power calculation.
- Follow relevant EQUATOR Network reporting guidance; randomized-trial protocols follow SPIRIT with the flow diagram in the main text and the populated checklist as an additional file.
- Report effect sizes, 95% confidence intervals, and statistical software with version as the default; these are not itemized in the JBC Instructions.

---

## Figures

- **Maximum**: No combined Original Article maximum stated.
- **Resolution**: 300 dpi for color, 900 dpi for grayscale, 1,200 dpi for black-and-white.
- **Format**: MS PowerPoint, JPG, or TIF.
- **Size**: 3.4 inches (7.5 x 10.0 cm) or 5.7 inches (12.5 x 17.5 cm).
- **Color**: Permitted. Combine related panels as Figure 1A, Figure 1B.
- **Other**: Report staining method and magnification for light microscopy; report reduced scale for electron microscopy. Define abbreviations below each table/figure and use the symbol order *, dagger, double dagger, section, parallel, paragraph.

## Common Rejection Reasons

_Editorial-risk summary drawn from the official instructions._

1. Out-of-scope manuscript without a clear breast-cancer, benign-breast-disease, or clinically useful breast-health focus.
2. Insufficient originality, or concerns about duplicate publication, plagiarism, image manipulation, or data integrity (CrossCheck screening is applied).
3. Failure to blind the manuscript, or title-page separation errors under double-blind review.
4. Missing IRB approval, informed-consent, or clinical-trial-registration statements where required.
5. Noncompliance with article-type limits or required structure, particularly the 4,000-word Original Article limit, the 300-word structured abstract, and the fifty-reference limit.

---

## Cover Letter

The Instructions do not prescribe an initial-submission cover-letter template. If included, it should state:
- Article type and why the work fits JBC's breast-cancer scope.
- Originality and that the manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere.
- Ethics, IRB, consent, and trial-registration status as applicable.
- Funding and conflicts of interest.
- For revised manuscripts, a cover letter with a brief description of the revisions is required, plus a separate point-by-point response to all reviewer comments.

## Positioning

Submit here for breast cancer-focused clinical, epidemiologic, translational, and surgical studies with a clear clinical message. It is particularly suitable when breast specificity and relevance to Korean or Asian practice are strengths, and for well-conducted single-center or multicenter cohorts that would face an importance ceiling at flagship oncology venues.

Do not prioritize JBC when the central contribution is a broad pan-cancer finding, a practice-changing international randomized trial, or a manuscript whose main topic is not breast disease.
