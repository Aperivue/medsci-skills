# Journal Profile: Applied Clinical Informatics

## Journal Identity

- **Full name**: Applied Clinical Informatics
- **Abbreviation**: Appl Clin Inform (ACI)
- **Publisher**: Georg Thieme Verlag KG
- **ISSN**: 1869-0327 (online; no separate print ISSN is listed by the publisher)
- **Frequency**: Quarterly (4 issues per year)
- **Impact Factor**: 2.7 (JCR 2025)
- **Open Access**: Hybrid — subscription journal with an optional article processing charge for open access; AMIA members receive a 50% discount on open-access fees and journal reviewers receive 15%. There are no submission charges. All articles are sent to PubMed Central, with a 12-month embargo before free full text is available unless the open-access option is chosen.
- **Acceptance rate**: Not published in the author guidelines
- **Peer review**: Double-anonymized (double-blinded) — authors submit an anonymized version for review and a non-anonymized version for publication; the Managing Editor checks blinding before peer review and returns inadequately blinded papers without review.

## Manuscript Types and Word Limits

Word limits are body limits stated in the author instructions; the instructions do not state a reference or figure limit per type except where noted below. Titles are limited to 20 words for every type.

| Type | Body Word Limit | Abstract | References | Figures/Tables |
|------|----------------|----------|------------|----------------|
| Research Article | Generally not to exceed 3,500 | Up to 300 words | AMA style, sequential; no per-type limit stated | No per-type limit stated |
| Review | Generally not to exceed 4,000 | Up to 300 words | AMA style, sequential; no per-type limit stated | No per-type limit stated |
| State of the Art / Best Practice Paper | Generally not to exceed 4,000 | Up to 300 words | AMA style, sequential; no per-type limit stated | No per-type limit stated |
| Brief Scientific Communication | Not to exceed 2,000 | Up to 250 words | AMA style, sequential; no per-type limit stated | Maximum of 3 figures or tables combined |
| Case Report | Not to exceed 2,000 (short reports preferred) | Up to 300 words | AMA style, sequential; no per-type limit stated | No per-type limit stated |
| Letter to the Editor | Not to exceed 1,500 | Not required | AMA style, sequential; no per-type limit stated | No more than one table or figure |
| Invited Editorial | Not to exceed 1,500 | Not required | AMA style, sequential; no per-type limit stated | No per-type limit stated |

State of the Art / Best Practice Papers are generally solicited contributions describing the state of the art in an area of clinical informatics, geared to lessons learned, best approaches, and safety and quality considerations; a systematic literature review is not required for this type. Editorials may be solicited and are not required to have an abstract, multiple-choice questions, a conflict-of-interest section, a human-subjects-protection section, or a clinical-relevance section.

## Abstract Requirements

Structured abstract, on a separate page, up to 300 words (250 words for Brief Scientific Communications). Headings: Background (optional), Objectives, Methods, Results, Conclusions. Keywords on a separate line below the abstract: up to 10 per the article-type table, using Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) terms; at least 2 keywords must be selected from the menu provided by ACI. Note: the abstract section of the instructions also states "no more than 5" keywords — the table value (10) is used in the table above and this discrepancy should be checked at submission.

## Required Sections (Original Article)

Research papers are organized into the following, in order:

1. **Title page** — concise and informative title; author list with affiliations; corresponding-author section with name, institution, address, country, ORCID, and email. The submitting author must provide an ORCID; other authors are recommended to link theirs.
2. **Abstract** — structured, as above.
3. **Background and Significance** — numbered heading 1.
4. **Objectives** — numbered heading 2.
5. **Methods** — numbered heading 3; includes human-subjects protections language (IRB/ethics review or a statement that no human or animal subjects were included; Helsinki compliance for human-subjects work; trial registration where applicable).
6. **Results** — numbered heading 4.
7. **Discussion** — numbered heading 5.
8. **Conclusions** — numbered heading 6.
9. **Clinical Relevance Statement** — a short paragraph of no more than 3 succinct sentences on the implications of the results for practitioners and/or consumers, placed following the Conclusions and before the References.
10. **Multiple Choice Questions** — 2-4 questions pertinent to the paper's content, placed after the Conclusions. Each question has a short stem (no negation words such as "not", "least likely", or "wrong"), four answers of similar structure of which one is clearly correct, with the correct answer indicated and an explanation of 1-3 paragraphs.
11. **Acknowledgments** — scientific advice, technical assistance, and financial support/materials, grouped at the end of the text (immediately after the Protection of Human and Animal Subjects section); blinded before submission, unblinded for accepted manuscripts.
12. **Conflict of Interest** — every author must disclose all financial and personal relationships that could bias the work, or state that there are none (for example: "The authors declare that they have no conflicts of interest in the research").
13. **Protection of Human and Animal Subjects** — as in Methods above.
14. **Appendices** — permitted; each appendix contains only one media type (text, images, or tables), listed after the body of the paper and before the references, and mentioned in the text.
15. **References** — AMA style with one Thieme exception (do not italicize book titles or journal title abbreviations, and do not put a period at the end of a reference); cited sequentially in the text with superscript numbers; list all author names up to six, and for more than six list the first three followed by et al. A page break separates the end of the text from the references.
16. **Figure captions** — written after the reference list, with a page break between references and captions; figures themselves are uploaded as separate files, never embedded in the manuscript.
17. **Tables** — placed after the figure captions as Word tables, double-spaced, numbered in citation order, with a short descriptive title; table data should be commented on but not repeated in the text.

Manuscript format: double-spaced, 12-point Times New Roman, 1-inch margins, American English, SI units (nonmetric equivalents in parentheses where helpful), generic drug names, no vendor-specific language. ACI's style guide reserves the word "impact" for collisions of bodies of mass — use "effect" or "affect" instead. Blinding: mask all identifying information (author names/initials, institutions/sites, self-citations framed as the authors' own work, traceable funding numbers) by replacing it with "XXX", and submit both an anonymized and a non-anonymized version identical in content apart from the masking.

## Figures

- **Maximum**: Brief Scientific Communications — 3 figures or tables combined; Letters to the Editor — no more than one table or figure; no per-type maximum is stated for other types in the guidelines text consulted.
- **Resolution**: 300 dpi for black-and-white and color artwork (TIFF); line art 1,200 dpi (EPS or TIFF). Lower resolutions and JPEG format for grayscale/color artwork are strongly discouraged.
- **Format**: Each figure saved as its own separate file (.TIF or .PNG for submission); do not submit art created in Microsoft Excel, Word, or PowerPoint. Figures must be cited sequentially in the text and numbered in citation order; captions follow the reference list.
- **Color**: Color artwork saved in CMYK, not RGB; do not send color art for conversion to black-and-white — convert it yourself first. Labels: one or two sizes of a serif font, 1-point or thicker rules and leader lines, arrows/asterisks/arrowheads white on dark areas and black on light areas.
- **Tables**: Word tables at the end of the manuscript (after figure captions), double-spaced, numbered in citation order; abbreviations used in a table are explained in a footnote at the end of the table; borrowed or adapted tables carry a credit line as the first footnote beneath the table.

## Common Rejection Reasons

1. Out of scope — the manuscript is not applied or translational clinical informatics: it does not concern information systems used in patient care (clinical, administrative, eHealth, mobile health), their development, deployment, evaluation, or socio-technical aspects, or it reports a methods advance with no applied or practical component.
2. Inadequate blinding — identifying information (author or institution names, self-citations, traceable funding) is not masked to "XXX", or only one manuscript version is submitted; the Managing Editor returns inadequately blinded papers without review.
3. Missing ACI-specific sections — no Clinical Relevance Statement (no more than 3 sentences, after the Conclusions), or no 2-4 multiple-choice questions with answers and explanations.
4. Format failures — manuscript not double-spaced in 12-point Times New Roman with 1-inch margins, figures embedded in the manuscript file instead of uploaded separately, or references not in sequential AMA style.
5. Prior-publication or preprint mishandling — the work is published, submitted, or posted elsewhere beyond the allowed preprint/thesis/abstract exceptions, or a preprint deposit is not noted in the cover letter, not described in terms of the adjustments made since deposition, and not cited (with DOI) as a reference.
6. Style non-compliance — vendor-specific language, or "impact" used where "effect" or "affect" is required by ACI's style guide.

## Cover Letter

The instructions name one explicit cover-letter requirement. If the manuscript was deposited as a preprint, the cover letter must note the preprint repository, state what adjustments and/or updates the draft has undergone between deposition and submission, and the preprint must be cited (including its DOI) as a reference in the manuscript. No other cover-letter contents are specified in the guidelines text consulted. Preprints are encouraged in initial-draft form (Research Square, arXiv, medRxiv, degree theses, and author/institutional websites); after submission and until a final decision, authors are discouraged from depositing new preprint versions, and 12 months after publication the accepted manuscript may be posted to the preprint.

## AI Writing Disclosure Policy

ACI's own author instructions (updated June 12, 2026) contain no AI-policy section; the applicable policy is Thieme's publisher-level policy, which aligns with the COPE Position Statement on Artificial Intelligence and Authorship.

- **Requirement level:** Required — a Declaration of Generative AI Use is mandatory at submission. If no generative AI was used, the statement reads: "The authors confirm that no Generative AI was used in the preparation of this manuscript."
- **Permitted scope:** Generative AI may support the work, but must not replace human expertise, critical reasoning, or scholarly judgment; use must always involve human oversight, review, and control. Authors are accountable and liable for every part of the paper, must carefully review and verify all AI-generated content (including checking cited sources, which may be biased, inaccurate, or fabricated), and must substantially edit and refine AI-assisted material so the manuscript reflects the authors' own contribution.
- **Disclosure location:** A dedicated "Declaration of Generative AI Use" statement submitted as part of the manuscript, stating which generative AI was used and for what purpose; the statement appears in the published article.
- **AI-generated images:** Generally not permitted in Thieme Science journals — research-integrity, privacy, and copyright issues for AI-generated visual content remain unresolved. Authors with a legitimate reason to include an AI-generated image for a specific purpose should contact the publishing editor; if approved, signing the "Publication of AI-generated images" form is required. Non-generative machine-learning tools may be used to enhance existing images if the use is disclosed in the caption.
- **Policy URL:** https://www.thieme.com/en-us/journal-policies (publisher-level policy; no journal-specific AI policy page)

## Positioning

Submit here when the contribution is applied clinical informatics in practice: a deployed system, its evaluation, implementation experience, or lessons learned (successes and failures) from health IT in care, aimed at clinical medicine and health IT specialists, CIOs, CMIOs, and informatics practitioners. Do not submit here when the paper's contribution is a general methods advance with no applied or deployment component, or a clinical finding that only happens to use software — those belong in a methods or clinical journal.
